Saving advice

How to find voucher codes that actually work

The best codes almost always come from the brand itself. Here's where to look before you pay full price.

By the VoucherVault editorial team · 16 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: charlesdeluvio on Unsplash

On this page

  1. 1. The banner at the top of the store
  2. 2. The brand's own offers page
  3. 3. Welcome offers
  4. 4. Group discounts
  5. Traps to avoid

1. The banner at the top of the store

Brands advertise their current codes in the strip at the top of their homepage. When we checked in October 2026, PUMA US showed a multi-buy code, Old Navy an extra 20% off clearance, and LOOKFANTASTIC a 20% code on selected beauty. It's the first place to look.

2. The brand's own offers page

Many brands have an official page listing promotions with end dates: LOOKFANTASTIC has a discount-codes page, Under Armour a promo-offers page, Nike a page explaining its member discounts, and Dell a coupons and offers page. These are the most reliable sources there are.

3. Welcome offers

Signing up for emails, texts or an app often unlocks a first-order discount: 15% at Under Armour and Gymshark, $10 for new UNIQLO app members, and 15% for joining The North Face's free XPLR Pass. See are sign-up discounts worth it?

4. Group discounts

Students, teachers, the military, first responders, healthcare workers and seniors often get 10–30% off, verified once through services like SheerID, ID.me, UNiDAYS or Student Beans. See student discounts and key worker discounts.

Traps to avoid

  • "Exclusive" codes that need you to install something. You don't need an extension to save money.
  • Coupon browser extensions deserve care: in late 2024 PayPal's Honey faced lawsuits after a YouTube investigation alleged it swapped in its own affiliate tags. Check what any extension can read and change.
  • Too-good-to-be-true codes (70% off everything) are almost always fake.
  • Phishing: never enter passwords or card details on a page you reached from a "free voucher" message.
